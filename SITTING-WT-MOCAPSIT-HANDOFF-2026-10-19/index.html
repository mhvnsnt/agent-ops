    WHAT THIS TAKE IS (plain language)¶

    This take gets a real human's sitting motion onto our character, instead of someone hand-posing every joint.

    The approach: start from a public library of real recorded human movement (Carnegie Mellon's motion-capture set — tiny sensors-on-a-suit recordings of a real person moving, here clip 113_15: a person sitting down in a chair and standing back up). Then a script (mocap_retarget.py, and the production version at production/tools/mocap-retarget/retarget.py) copies that person's joint angles onto Cape Jones's 94-bone rig — his full digital skeleton. "Retargeting" just means: the recorded person and our character have different skeletons, so the script translates the motion from one body to the other.

    The tricky part the lane solved: it also fixes where he lands. Without help, copying the motion would park him two meters in the air or sink him through the chair. The script numerically locks his body so his butt lands exactly on the chair seat (measured: 0.00mm gap — touching, not floating), his feet rest on the floor, and his arms move in the natural gesture from the recording rather than sticking out stiff (the "H-pose" you get when only half the body is posed). One hand-tweak was deliberately kept: the recording had the actor fidgeting his head around, so head and neck motion is damped to half (50%), which the README frames as selecting channels, not hand-posing.

    What it does well: it's real human motion — 81 frames of an actual person sitting down and standing up, so it avoids the puppet-on-strings look of posed animation. Contact is exact (butt on seat, feet on floor). Two stills are in the tree (proof/mocap-sit/sit_front.png, proof/mocap-sit/sit_threequarter.png) plus the animated result (proof/sitting_mocap.blend) so you can see it before deciding anything.

    What's rough/unfinished: only one clip was finished (the "sit in chair and get up" clip); a list of six more sitting clips exists but was never pulled into the repo. The math the script uses is fussy — the README warns that using the wrong rotation reference produces garbage poses (thighs backward, chest sideways), which was the bug in earlier versions; anyone re-running it needs to follow the README exactly. The script hard-codes the seat position it was tuned for, so it isn't a general "sit anywhere" button yet. And it solves sitting contact only — it doesn't handle the chair-back, table, or the mug/props.

    How it feels different from a generic sitting setup: a generic setup poses the body by hand and it looks posed. This one looks like a person because it IS a person — the recording carries the little weight-shifts and timing a human makes when sitting down. That's the whole bet of this take.
